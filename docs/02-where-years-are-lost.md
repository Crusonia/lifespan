# 02 · Where the healthy years are lost

Before ranking any lever, decompose the shortfall. If you don't know where the years go, you can't know which lever recovers them.

## The size of the gap

| Measure | US | Comparable wealthy countries | Gap | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Life expectancy at birth | ~79.0 (2024) | ~82–83 | ~3–4 years | NCHS; OECD |
| Lifespan minus healthy lifespan | ~12.4 years | ~9–10 years | ~2–3 years more time sick | Garmany et al. 2024 |
| Life expectancy, top vs bottom 1% income (age 40) | 14.6 years (men), 10.1 (women) | n/a | Within-US gap larger than the international gap | Chetty et al., JAMA 2016 |

Two conclusions follow, and both shape the rest of the benchmark.

1. **The internal gap is bigger than the international one.** The distance between the healthiest and least healthy Americans is three to four times the distance between the US average and its peers. "For all" is not a slogan. It is where most of the recoverable years are.
2. **The US problem is as much time spent sick as early death.** A plan that only extends life without extending health moves S1's companion (life expectancy) and not S1 itself.

## Decomposition by cause

Chronic disease is the dominant term.

- About 6 in 10 US adults have at least one chronic disease and 4 in 10 have two or more. About 90% of national health spending goes to people with chronic and mental health conditions. [CDC]
- About 38 million Americans have diabetes and about 98 million adults have prediabetes, most of whom don't know it. [CDC National Diabetes Statistics Report]
- Cardiovascular disease, cancer, chronic respiratory disease, diabetes and kidney disease account for the majority of deaths and of years lived with disability. [US Burden of Disease Collaborators, JAMA 2018]

## Decomposition by driver

This is the decomposition that matters for levers. The best-known estimates put the share of premature death attributable to medical care at roughly 10–15%. Behavior, environment and social circumstances account for most of the rest. [McGinnis, Williams-Russo & Knickman, Health Affairs 2002; Schroeder, NEJM 2007]

The US Burden of Disease study ranked **dietary risk as the leading risk factor for death in the United States**, ahead of tobacco. [US Burden of Disease Collaborators, JAMA 2018]

| Driver | Rough share of premature death | Main channel | Main financing debate touches it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Behavior and diet | ~40% | Metabolic and cardiovascular disease, cancer | Barely |
| Genetics | ~30% | Interacts with all of the above | No |
| Social and environmental circumstances | ~20% | Income, housing, food access, pollution | Indirectly |
| Medical care | ~10% | Access, quality, timeliness | **Yes, almost exclusively** |

*These shares are old, approximate and contested. They are orders of magnitude, not parameters. A priority for the [evidence agenda](08-evidence-agenda.md) is to replace them with a modern decomposition.*

## The implication

The financing debate is concentrated on the channel that accounts for roughly a tenth of premature death. That does not make care unimportant. Access to care saves lives at the margin, and for some conditions (acute, infectious, cancers caught early) it is decisive. It does mean that **a benchmark that only scores care delivery is scoring the smallest lever.**

The rest of the benchmark is built so that every channel, care included, competes on the same scale.

## The metabolic pathway as the first modeled channel

The first channel modeled in [05 · Model](06-model.md) is metabolic health: the transition from healthy to insulin resistance and prediabetes to type 2 diabetes. It was chosen first because:

- it is large (roughly a third of adults are in the prediabetic state);
- it is upstream of cardiovascular, kidney and several other diseases;
- it is measurable with cheap tests (HbA1c, fasting glucose, triglyceride-to-HDL ratio);
- it is partly reversible, especially in the prediabetic ("PreChronic") stage;
- food and GLP-1 drugs both act on it, so it puts the two most-debated non-coverage levers side by side.
