# 04 · The levers

A lever is anything that changes the flows in [03](03-system-dynamics.md). Each one is listed with where it acts, its best evidence, the grade of that evidence ([S5](01-scoreboard.md)) and its delay. Nothing is ranked by belief. Ranking waits for the [model](06-model.md).

## Upstream levers (act on Healthy → PreChronic)

| Lever | Acts on | Best evidence | Grade | Delay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shift diets away from ultra-processed food** | Onset of insulin resistance, weight gain | Inpatient RCT: ultra-processed diet drove ~500 extra kcal/day and weight gain vs unprocessed (Hall et al., Cell Metab 2019). Large cohorts link UPF share to mortality and T2D. | B for intake/weight; C for disease outcomes | Weeks (intake) to years (disease) |
| **Price and access to nutrient-dense food** (produce prescriptions, incentives, procurement) | Diet quality in low-income groups | Mostly observational and pre/post; some small trials on HbA1c. Rapidly growing state programs, mostly unmeasured. | C | Months to years |
| **Taxes on sugar-sweetened beverages** | Purchases of sugary drinks | Natural experiments: Philadelphia ~38% volume decline in taxed drinks, partly offset by cross-border buying. | B for purchases; C for disease | Months |
| **Personal metabolic measurement** (cheap panels, CGM, wearables) | Awareness → behavior change | Feedback improves engagement; durable effect on outcomes not established. | C/D | Months |

## Mid-stream levers (act on PreChronic → Healthy and PreChronic → Chronic)

| Lever | Acts on | Best evidence | Grade | Delay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Find PreChronic early** (universal HbA1c / fasting glucose screening) | Diagnosis rate of the ~98M with prediabetes | Screening alone does little; its value depends on what follows. | Enabler (graded by the follow-on lever) | Immediate |
| **Intensive lifestyle programs** (Diabetes Prevention Program model) | Progression to T2D | RCT: 58% lower diabetes incidence vs placebo at ~3 years (DPP, NEJM 2002); ~27% lower at 15 years (DPPOS). | A | 1–3 years |
| **Medically tailored meals / food as medicine** | Diet in people already sick | Observational: fewer admissions and lower costs; RCT evidence still thin and mixed. | C | Months |
| **GLP-1 and related drugs** | Weight, glycemia, cardiovascular events | RCTs: large weight loss; 20% fewer major cardiovascular events in people with obesity and CVD (SELECT, NEJM 2023). High cost; most stop within a year. | A for treated; persistence is the open question | Months, reverses on stopping |

## Downstream levers (act on Chronic → Death, and access)

| Lever | Acts on | Best evidence | Grade | Delay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Health insurance coverage** | Access, financial protection, some mortality | Medicaid expansion reduced mortality in low-income near-elderly adults (Miller, Johnson & Wherry, QJE 2021; Goldin, Lurie & McCubbin, QJE 2021). Oregon experiment: more care used, less depression and financial strain, no significant change in measured BP, cholesterol or HbA1c in 2 years. | A for financial protection; B for mortality in specific groups | 1–5 years |
| **Lower cost-sharing for high-value care** | Use of effective care | RAND HIE: cost-sharing cut use ~30% with little average health effect, but worse blood pressure control for poor, sick patients. | A | 1–3 years |
| **Care quality and chronic-disease management** | Complications, mortality | Mixed; strong for specific interventions (BP control, statins), weak for many care-management programs. | A–C by intervention | 1–5 years |

## Enabling levers (make the others possible)

| Lever | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- |
| **Pay for healthy years** (outcome contracts measured on the scoreboard) | Breaks the "paid for disease" and "wrong pocket" loops |
| **Long-term accountability** (multi-year enrollment or shared savings that follow the person) | Lets whoever pays for prevention capture its return |
| **Open measurement** (common outcome definitions, public data) | Lets buyers compare and pay for what works |

## What the table already says

1. **Most of the grade-A evidence is mid- and downstream.** The upstream levers likely have the largest potential reach, and the weakest evidence on hard outcomes. That is the central uncertainty of this benchmark, and it is stated rather than hidden.
2. **The PreChronic stage has the best combination of size, reversibility and evidence.** The DPP is one of the strongest prevention results in medicine, and it is barely deployed.
3. **Coverage and food are not rivals.** They act on different flows. A plan can use both. The benchmark scores the mix.
